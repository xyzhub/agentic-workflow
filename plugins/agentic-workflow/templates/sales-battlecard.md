# {{PROJECT_NAME}} — Battlecard

Head-to-head against the real alternatives — **including the status quo and the
build-it-yourself option**, which are the alternatives that win most deals. One
card per alternative: where *they* win, where *we* win, the trap questions that
expose the difference, and the landmines to avoid. Sourced from the V0 competitor
table in `idea.md`; claims trace to `positioning.md`. This is an internal
enablement doc — it is honest about where a competitor is genuinely better, never
a hit piece.

## How to read a card

- **They win when** — the buyer situations where the honest recommendation is the
  alternative. Naming these keeps you credible and stops you chasing bad-fit deals.
- **We win when** — the situations where {{PROJECT_NAME}}'s differentiator (from
  `positioning.md`) decides it. Lead here only when the buyer is actually in one.
- **Trap questions** — open questions that surface the difference *without*
  trashing the competitor; the buyer reaches the conclusion themselves.
- **Landmines** — what NOT to do: claims that backfire, comparisons that aren't
  true, the competitor's genuine strengths you shouldn't pick a fight with.

## The status quo ("we do nothing / we cope today")

The alternative in most deals. Beating it is about the cost of inaction, not
features.

- **They win when:** the pain is real but tolerable, or the switching cost
  outweighs it this quarter. Don't manufacture urgency — if now isn't the time,
  it isn't.
- **We win when:** the "why now" trigger from `positioning.md` has fired — a cost
  that just grew, a tool that just broke, a deadline that just landed.
- **Trap questions:** _"What does coping with (the problem) cost you in a normal
  week?"_ · _"What happens the next time (the trigger) hits?"_
- **Landmines:** don't dismiss what they do today — it got them this far. Anchor
  on the *growing* cost, not on their past choice being wrong.

## Build-it-yourself (DIY / in-house)

- **They win when:** the team has spare capacity, the need is deeply bespoke, and
  maintenance isn't their bottleneck.
- **We win when:** time-to-value matters and the build isn't their core product —
  point at the since-version history in `feature-benefit-catalog.md` for what
  ships on day one.
- **Trap questions:** _"What would your team stop shipping to build this?"_ ·
  _"Who owns it the day after it's built?"_
- **Landmines:** never say it can't be built — it can. The argument is
  opportunity cost and maintenance, not capability.
