# {{PROJECT_NAME}} — Objections & FAQ

The catalogued objections a rep hears and the honest, evidence-backed way through
each. Every answer *concedes what's true*, then returns to evidence — it never
pushes, spins, or claims past what the product does. Claims trace to
`positioning.md`; facts and proof trace to `feature-benefit-catalog.md`; prices
reference `business/pricing.md`. If the honest answer is "not yet", say so — a
deferred capability is named as deferred, never dressed up.

## How to use this doc

Each row is a four-part move: the **objection** in the buyer's words, the
**honest answer** (concede the true part first), the **evidence** that backs it
(a proof-column citation, a `positioning.md` pillar, or a shipped behavior), and
the **pivot-back** line that returns the conversation to the buyer's problem. An
objection with no honest answer is a **product gap, not a script problem** — log
it for the roadmap rather than talking around it.

## Objection catalogue

| Objection (their words) | Honest answer (concede first) | Evidence | Pivot-back line |
|---|---|---|---|
| _"It's too expensive."_ | Name the value established in discovery before the number; the price is small beside the cost of inaction. | The cost-of-inaction figure the buyer gave you + the tier fit in `business/pricing.md`. | _"Set against what {{PROBLEM}} costs you each week, where does that land?"_ |
| _"We already do this with (incumbent / spreadsheet)."_ | Respect the status quo — it works, until it doesn't at scale. | The differentiator pillar from `positioning.md` + the proof-moment the incumbent can't match. | _"When (the trigger) happens, what does your current setup cost you to keep up?"_ |
| _"We'll just build it ourselves."_ | Fair — the parts are buildable; the question is whether it's the best use of your team. | The since-version history in the catalog: time-to-value you get on day one vs. a build. | _"What would your team stop shipping to build and maintain this?"_ |
| _"Does it do (capability we haven't shipped)?"_ | Not today — that's on the roadmap, not in this version. Say so plainly. | The PRD's "NOT in this version" list — name it as deferred, never implied. | _"For what you need now, here's what ships today that solves (the live problem)."_ |
| _"How do I know it actually works?"_ | You shouldn't take the claim on faith — see it run. | The demo-moment from the catalog; the measured numbers in `proof-points.md` (unmeasured stays unmeasured). | _"Let's run the exact path you'd use it for, live."_ |
| _"Now isn't the right time."_ | Timing is real; there's no manufactured deadline here. | The "why now" trigger from `positioning.md` — a real change in their world, not invented urgency. | _"What would have to be true for this to be worth solving this quarter?"_ |

_Seed rows are **illustrative** — replace `{{PROBLEM}}` and each row with this
venture's real objections as they surface in calls. Delete the examples once real
objections land._

## The rules every answer obeys

- **Concede the true part first.** An answer that denies a real limitation loses
  trust; one that names it and pivots keeps it.
- **No claim past the evidence.** If it isn't in `positioning.md` or the catalog's
  proof column, it isn't asserted here — see the `writer`'s anti-manipulation rule
  (no fabricated urgency, no confirm-shaming).
- **Deferred is deferred.** A capability the product doesn't have yet is answered
  as "not yet", pointing at what *does* ship — never implied into existence.
- **Prices reference, never restate.** Point at `business/pricing.md`; a quoted
  number here would go stale.
